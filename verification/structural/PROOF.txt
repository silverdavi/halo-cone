1. TASK A: A CLOSED-FORM ROBUST SEPARATOR
--------------------------------------

1.1 General sufficient lemma.

Let H_i>0 be C^2 near x0, let ell_i=log H_i, and let nu>0 be C^2 there. Set
  s_i=ell_i'(x0), t_i=ell_i''(x0)+s_i^2,
  a=max_i(-2s_i), b=max_i t_i,
  D=max_i s_i-min_i s_i, T=max_i ell_i''-min_i ell_i'',
  B=nu'/nu, C=nu''/nu at x0.
Thus min s_i=-a/2, max s_i=-a/2+D.

If D>0, T>0, p=a+D>=0, and
  G = C-b + (a+D)(B+a/2-D) - T > 0,                 (1)
choose, in closed form,
  p=a+D,   r=-b+(a+D)(a/2-D)-T.                    (2)
Then, at x0,
  2s_i+p >= D >0,
  t_i+p s_i+r <= -T <0,
  (Lnu)/nu=G>0,    L=d^2/dx^2+p d/dx+r.           (3)
These inequalities hold on some neighborhood of x0, with p,r constant.

Proof. The first bound follows from s_i>=-a/2. For the second, use t_i<=b,
s_i<=-a/2+D, and p>=0. Substitute (2). The third is an identity. Continuity
and the strict margins give a common neighborhood for finitely many isotopes.

The positive-spread conditions in this convenient formula are not essential.
More generally, choose ANY positive slacks d,e and set
  p=max(a,0)+d, r=-b-p(-a/2+D)-e.
The same conclusion holds if C+pB+r>0, with strict isotope margins d,e.
This covers zero spreads, without falsely inferring a neighborhood from a
non-strict point inequality. Formula (2) uses d=D,e=T in the cases at hand.

For an isotropic halo, set g_i(x)=eta(lambda_i x). Whenever lambda_i x<a_E,
g_i>=0, g_i'<=0, g_i''<=0 distributionally. The product rule gives
  L(W_i g_i)=W_i[g_i''+(2ell_i'+p)g_i'
                         +(ell_i''+ell_i'^2+p ell_i'+r)g_i] <=0.          (4)
Summing gives LD<=0, contradicting D=positive_constant*nu and Lnu>0.

At threshold E0, all isotope speeds are below a_E whenever
  m>max_i m_hi,i(E0),
  m_hi,i(E0)=M_i mu_i/(M_i-mu_i),
  mu_i=c_light sqrt(M_i E0*10^-6/2)/a_E.
For each such m take a sufficiently short interval immediately above E0,
also below the specified upper cutoff. The maximum is certified at 136Xe.
Hence (1) proves the stated upper-mass exclusion for every E_cut>E0.
It proves a necessary upper bound; it does not prove that the bound is attained.

1.2 Exact feasibility test and displacement caveat.

The actual pointwise coefficient region is unbounded; it is a polyhedron,
not necessarily a bounded polytope. It is
  p>=a, r<=-t_i-p s_i for every i.
It intersects {Lnu>0} if and only if
  there is p>=a with f(p)=min_i[C-t_i+p(B-s_i)]>0.     (5)
Necessity and sufficiency follow by eliminating r. This is an exact
one-dimensional concave, piecewise-affine feasibility criterion. It can be
checked at p=a and all pairwise line intersections p>=a, plus the unbounded
direction: B>max s_i makes f grow to +infinity. Zero-slope final pieces are
already covered by their finite endpoints. If B<=min s_i, f is nonincreasing
and feasibility is simply f(a)>0. All ten requested cases satisfy this last
condition. Their exact pointwise optimal gaps are included in results.json.

Failure of (1), a sufficient bound using summaries, would not prove (5)
infeasible. Failure at one old pair (p,r) certainly does not prove that.
Here both the closed-form strict witness and the exact criterion certify
feasibility, so there is no infeasible requested model to repair.

Finding a separator only at a later E1>E_thr would not automatically recover
the original threshold mass bound. It excludes masses for which all speeds
are below a_E at E1, generally giving max m_hi,i(E1), a weaker bound. For
m approaching the original upper bound from above, the available concavity
interval shrinks to the threshold. Feasibility arbitrarily close to the
threshold, or another argument covering those masses, is required.

1.4 Eliminating the Helm functional form completely.

Assume F_i is the normalized Fourier transform of a nonnegative spherical
spatial probability distribution with second moment R2_i=<r^2>_i. At a fixed
direction it can be written F_i(x)=E[cos(x Z_i)], with
  t_i^mom=E[Z_i^2]=2 M_i*10^-6 R2_i/(3(hbar*c)^2).
Finite second moment gives C^2 regularity by dominated differentiation, and
  F_i >= 1-t_i^mom x^2/2,
  |F_i'| <= t_i^mom |x|, |F_i''| <= t_i^mom.          (6)
The first inequality is cos y>=1-y^2/2; the derivative bounds use
|sin y|<=|y| and |cos y|<=1. No fourth moment or Taylor remainder is needed.

Let t=max_i t_i^mom and f0=1-t x^2/2>0. Uniform bounds on a short interval are
  beta1=2t |x|/f0,
  beta2=2t/f0+2(t x/f0)^2,                            (7)
with their suprema over that interval understood. Then |ell_i'|<=beta1 and
|ell_i''|<=beta2. Set the CONSTANTS
  p=3 beta1, r=-beta2-5 beta1^2.                      (8)
The isotope margins are at least beta1 and beta1^2. For an arbitrary given
target, C+3 beta1 B-beta2-5 beta1^2>0 suffices.

There is also a condition independent of the detailed target form factors.
For k_i=sqrt(M_i/2), set psi_i(x)=Psi(k_i x). Since psi_i'<0,
  L(H_i psi_i)/H_i >=
     k_i^2 Psi'' - 5 beta1 k_i |Psi'|
                      -(2 beta2+8 beta1^2)Psi.       (9)
Indeed 2ell_i'+p<=5beta1, and the zero-order coefficient is bounded below
by -beta2-p beta1+r=-2beta2-8beta1^2. Positive isotope weights can be summed.

The script proves (9)/Psi>0 for EVERY isotope with the following caps:
  E_thr=.51: all R2_i<=69 fm^2, margin >.02076 keV^(-1);
  E_thr=.30: all R2_i<=44 fm^2, margin >.007549 keV^(-1).
The caps imply f0>.9618 and >.9856 respectively. These margins cover the
entire stated 10^-10 keV neighborhood. Exact uniform beta constants and p,r
are in results.json. They are deliberately conservative.

The nominal nine moments range from 22.1083831183 to 23.4040356552 fm^2,
with spread 1.2956525369 fm^2. A sufficient admissible spread, with the
minimum moment fixed at the nominal lightest value, is
  0 <= max R2_i-min R2_i <=46.8916168817 fm^2 at .51,
  0 <= max R2_i-min R2_i <=21.8916168817 fm^2 at .30,
provided the respective maximum-moment cap is respected. More generally the
right side is cap-min R2_i. This is NOT a claim that spread alone, without
an absolute-radius scale, controls the derivatives. Even zero spread can
have arbitrarily large common radii.

If only H=1-cx^2+O(x^4) is asserted, without a positive-density hypothesis or
a quantitative remainder bound, no finite derivative bound follows at x0>0.
For example ell(x)=-c x^2+epsilon b(x) cos(n(x-x0)), with a smooth bump b
supported away from zero and b=1 near x0, leaves every coefficient at zero
unchanged and H=exp(ell)>0, while ell''(x0) contains -epsilon n^2. Reversing
the sign gives arbitrarily large positive curvature. Thus the unqualified
Taylor-only version of the requested assertion is false; (6) supplies the
additional physical hypothesis that makes a moment-only theorem valid.


2. TASK B: UNEQUAL WEAK AND MASS FORM FACTORS
-------------------------------------------

2.1 Perturbation lemma.

Let rho>0 be C^2 on a q interval K in (q_*,U), and put
  eta_nu(w)=constant*rho(q)Psi(q), q=kappa w,
  kappa=1000 mu_N/c_light MeV/(km/s).
If pointwise bounds |rho'/rho|<=b1(q), |rho''/rho|<=b2(q) satisfy
  4 b1 h+b2 Psi < 2(-h'),                            (10)
then eta_nu is strictly convex on K mapped to w. This follows immediately
from
  (rho Psi)''/rho=Psi''+2(rho'/rho)Psi'+(rho''/rho)Psi
              >=2(-h')-4 b1 h-b2 Psi>0.
The dimensionless loss is
  R(q)=[2 b1 |Psi'|+b2 Psi]/Psi''.                   (11)
Bounds b1,b2 may be constants, but the test should retain the q dependence
of Psi, Psi', Psi''. If rho'<=0 the mixed term is favorable and can instead
be kept with its actual sign. No such sign assumption is needed here.

An explicit sufficient derivative box, independent of any skin model, is
  |rho'/rho|<=.00485 MeV^(-1), |rho''/rho|<=.00051 MeV^(-2)
throughout [q_*+.1,U). The interval calculation gives R<.992712 before the
last segment; the analytic last-segment bound is .000194102. Thus any
positive C^2 ratio satisfying this box preserves strict convexity there.

For monotonicity, separately require b1 Psi<|Psi'| when no sign of rho' is
known. Convexity alone is enough for the upper-bound necessity argument;
full single-isotope sufficiency also requires decreasing eta_nu.

2.2 A specified skin model, and what the literature input means.

The proton rms inputs, in isotope order, are
  4.766,4.774,4.777,4.777,4.781,4.780,4.785,4.789,4.796 fm.
They are the nominal proton-radius entries of Table I of Aristizabal Sierra,
Liao and Marfatia, arXiv:1902.07398, JHEP 06 (2019) 141. That paper uses
0--0.3 fm as an envelope of neutron-radius models for xenon, rather than an
experimental determination of its skin. We treat these rounded radii and
the range as exact hypotheses, not as measurements with certified coverage.

For the Helm test, F_p and F_n are separate normalized Helm amplitudes with
s=.9 fm and rms radii r_p and r_n=r_p+d, for the ENTIRE interval 0<=d<=.3 fm.
For isoscalar SI DM, with eps=1-4 sin^2(theta_W)=.04572, define
  F_W=(N F_n-eps Z F_p)/(N-eps Z),
  F_M=(N F_n+Z F_p)/A, rho=(F_W/F_M)^2.              (12)
The weak and mass radii are not identified with r_n and r_p separately.
This distinction and Qphys=2q are both necessary for a consistent skin test.

Arb evaluates derivatives analytically. The spherical factor has the stable
series
  3j1(z)/z=sum_{n>=0} (-1)^n 6(n+1) z^(2n)/(2n+3)!.
The first 20 terms and their first two derivatives are used. For |z|<2 and
n>=20, successive absolute tail terms have ratio <1/4, also after either
derivative; hence 4/3 of the first omitted term bounds each entire tail.
The Gaussian factor is differentiated analytically. These are certified
series enclosures, not truncated series treated as exact form factors.

The script covers q from 3.281534858300 to 16.34 in 653 rational cells and
d in the whole ball [0,.3] in every cell, for all nine isotopes. It proves
  maximum R(q) < .203,
  |rho'/rho| < .004809 MeV^(-1),
  |rho''/rho| < .000506 MeV^(-2),
where the derivative bounds also cover the final segment to U. These
global bounds are conservative; the loss bound uses the sharper
cell-specific bounds. At the final segment the
analytic argument in 2.5 gives loss <.000193. Thus 0.1--0.3 fm is included,
and convexity is certified on [q_*+.1,U), continuously reaching zero
curvature at U. This does not assert stability all the way down to q_*
for an arbitrary perturbation, where the unperturbed curvature vanishes.

On the requested compact interval the old margin is re-certified as
  -h' >=6.1162910145327...*10^-9 MeV^(-2)>6*10^-9.
Combining this tiny minimum with global maxima of h and Psi would give a
useless loss bound about 148455. That failure is a failure of this crude
estimate, not an infeasibility or loss of convexity. The pointwise scaled
bound (11) is <.203 and resolves the issue.

2.3 A local skin result without a Helm shape assumption.

Assume only nonnegative spherical proton and neutron densities with the
specified r_p and 0<=d<=.3. Define the squared-radius bounds
  R_W,abs^2=(N r_n^2+eps Z r_p^2)/Q,
  R_M^2=(N r_n^2+Z r_p^2)/A.                         (13)
The first uses the absolute coefficients in the signed weak amplitude.
For X=W,M, set t_X=4 R_X^2/(3(hbar*c in MeV fm)^2), f_X=1-t_X q^2/2,
u_X=t_X q/f_X, v_X=t_X/f_X. Where f_X>0, the characteristic-function bounds
give |F_X'/F_X|<=u_X, |F_X''/F_X|<=v_X. For the weak amplitude the lower
bound follows either from the absolute weights in (13), or the sharper
F_W>=1-N(1-F_n)/Q. Consequently one may take
  b1=2(u_W+u_M),
  b2=2(v_W+u_W^2+v_M+u_M^2)+b1^2.                  (14)
Indeed these bound the first log derivative of rho, then its second
log derivative plus the square of the first log derivative.

For each isotope, on q in [q_thr,i,q_thr,i+.01], the script proves:
  E_thr=.51: R(q)<.254218, b1<.020678, b2<.004169;
  E_thr=.30: R(q)<.433850, b1<.015772, b2<.003924.
Units of b1,b2 are respectively MeV^(-1), MeV^(-2). All are strict positive
curvature margins, and cover arbitrary shapes satisfying the stated density
and radius hypotheses. They do not rely on a small remainder of a low-q
expansion. The interval can be shortened as needed to stay inside J.

The mixture upper bound also survives, not merely the single-isotope one.
The maximum R_W,abs^2 in (13) is <27.489415 fm^2, and R_M^2 is smaller. Both
are below the caps in Section 1.4. Apply (6)--(9) to H_DM for the halo signs
and to H_W for target positivity, using the same uniform beta1,beta2. The
proof of (9) needs only those derivative bounds, not identical form factors.
This certifies the unchanged numerical upper-mass exclusions at .51 and .30.

For reference, the sufficient moment-operator skin cap is explicitly
  d_cap=min_i{sqrt[(Q_i Rcap^2-eps Z r_p,i^2)/N_i]-r_p,i}.
It is >3.34095 fm for Rcap^2=69 and >1.68340 fm for Rcap^2=44. These large
values describe slack in the conservative operator test, not plausible
physical skins or a global convexity theorem at such large skins. The
certified local convexity statement (14) here is for 0<=d<=.3 only.

  .51: -h'>.01204 MeV^(-2), q_thr in [5.4244311,5.6811214];
  .30: -h'>.00730 MeV^(-2), q_thr in [4.1603487,4.3572212].
Whenever J is nonempty, shorten this local interval to lie below q_E and
the detector cutoff. Its length may depend on m and may tend to zero as
m decreases to the upper edge. The strictly positive local margins do not.
Together with (14), they prove the skin-insensitive upper-bound necessity.
For eta'' in speed units multiply the q-curvature bound by kappa^2 and the
positive normalization and rho. No positive full-J margin is claimed.

2.5 What actually happens near U.

For q on the nonincreasing tail of phi, putting d=U-q gives
  Psi'' >=2 phi(q)/U,
  |Psi'| <=2 phi(q)d/U,
  Psi <=phi(q)d^2/U.
These follow from phi(t)<=phi(q), integration of 1/t^2, and
int_q^U(1-q^2/t^2)dt=d^2/U. Thus
  |Psi'|/Psi''<=d, Psi/Psi''<=d^2/2.
Bounded b1,b2 imply R(q)<=2b1 d+b2 d^2/2, which tends to zero at U.
This proves the last-segment assertion without dividing by a ball that
contains zero. On the actual final linear segment phi(U-d)=K d,
Psi~K d^3/(3U), Psi''~2K d/U, Psi''/Psi~6/d^2.
Absolute curvature vanishes but relative robustness improves. If rho or
its derivatives were singular at U, these conclusions would need separate
hypotheses; no such singularity occurs in the tested form factors.


3. TASK C(1): SUFFICIENCY, ENDPOINTS, AND ATOMS
---------------------------------------------

3.1 True endpoint.

Since phi(U)=0,
  h(U)=0, h'(U)=int_U^U phi/t^2-phi(U)/U=0.
Therefore Psi'(U)=Psi''(U)=0. For a positive C^2 ratio rho with finite
derivatives there, (rho Psi)'(U)=(rho Psi)''(U)=0 as well.

Let eta_nu be nonnegative, decreasing and convex on [w1,w2], w1>=a_E,
eta_nu(w2)=eta_nu'(w2)=0, w2<=S. Put q_nu=-2a_E eta_nu' and extend it by
zero for w>w2. Define
  R(v)=sum_{j>=0} q_nu(v+(2j+1)a_E), v>=w1-a_E,
and extend R constantly to smaller nonnegative v. The sum is finite.
It is nonnegative, nonincreasing, supported below w2-a_E<=V, and absolutely
continuous when q_nu is absolutely continuous and joins zero continuously.
Set dF(u)=-u dR(u). It is a finite nonnegative measure, absolutely continuous
with density -u R'(u); there is no atom at w2-a_E or its shifted copies.
Absolute continuity here refers to the measure/cumulative distribution;
its density need not be a continuous function at the lower support boundary.

For w>=w1, telescoping gives
  R(w-a_E)-R(w+a_E)=q_nu(w)=-2a_E eta_nu'(w).
Differentiating the shell integral gives exactly the same left-hand side.
The support makes eta(w2)=0; equality of derivatives and this value gives
eta=eta_nu throughout [w1,w2]. Normalize F and absorb its total mass in the
cross section. Thus the supplied single-isotope full-endpoint sufficiency
proof is valid under its stated decreasing/convex target assumptions.
The alleged downward jump at the true endpoint has size zero; the clause
about such a drop is vacuous there.

3.2 A cutoff: the proposed atom statement is false as a statement about F.

If instead w2 is a cutoff with eta_nu(w2)>0 and eta_nu'(w2)<0, artificially
extending q_nu by zero creates a downward jump
  J0=q_nu(w2^-)=-2a_E eta_nu'(w2^-)>0.
The R-series has jumps of size J0 at all
  u_j=w2-(2j+1)a_E
lying strictly above w1-a_E. Accordingly its speed measure has atoms
  F({u_j})=u_j J0=-2a_E u_j eta_nu'(w2^-).           (16)
In particular F({w2-a_E})=-(w2-a_E)2a_E eta_nu'(w2^-).
J0 alone is the mass of dF/u, equivalently the jump of R, not the mass of dF.
A shifted point at the lower interval boundary can be omitted: its upper
shell endpoint is w1 and it has no effect inside the measured interval.
When w2-w1<2a_E, only j=0 occurs. Otherwise there can be additional atoms.

This artificial construction matches the slope but has eta(w2)=0, hence
misses the target by the positive constant eta_nu(w2) on the whole interval.
It is NOT a sufficient cutoff construction. Nor is a cutoff atom required
of a correct solution: restricting any atom-free full-endpoint solution to
an earlier cutoff is an immediate counterexample to that assertion.

3.3 The terminal value-and-slope condition.

For w_c>V-a_E=312 km/s every contributing shell is on its decreasing branch,
  eta(w_c)=int_(w_c-a_E)^V (u+a_E-w_c)/(2a_E u) dF(u),
  -eta'(w_c)=int_(w_c-a_E)^V 1/(2a_E u) dF(u),
with suitable one-sided derivatives at atoms. Therefore
  eta(w_c) <= (S-w_c)(-eta'(w_c)).                   (17)
A positive target value forces a negative slope and more than bare support.
For eta_nu=constant*Psi(mu w) in consistent natural-speed units, this is
  mu >= [q_c+Psi(q_c)/(2h(q_c))]/S.
The positive value term disappears only at the true zero endpoint.

For a single isotope with a decreasing convex target on [w1,w2], w1>=a_E,
condition (17) is also a constructive sufficient terminal condition: extend
eta linearly with its terminal slope until
  w3=w2+eta(w2)/(-eta'(w2))<=S,
then set it to zero. The resulting extension is nonnegative, decreasing and
convex, and the R-series gives a realizing speed measure. When w2>312,
(17) is necessary as well. If the slope is zero while the value is positive,
no such tail exists in this high-speed regime.

For a mixture the required terminal data are coupled. With x_c=sqrt(E_cut)
and v_i=lambda_i x_c, a single eta must satisfy BOTH
  nu(x_c)=sum_i W_i(x_c) eta(v_i),
  nu_x(x_c)=sum_i W_i(x_c)[ell_i'(x_c)eta(v_i)+lambda_i eta'(v_i)].        (18)
It must simultaneously satisfy the entire interior functional equation and
the boosted-shell inversion constraints. There is no isotopewise division
producing nine independent freely selectable eta_i. Matching support, or
independently matching nine value/slope pairs, does not enforce (18) or the
common halo. A positive isotope sum at a cutoff needs at least one isotope
to contribute, not all nine. Thus even the claimed necessity of the maximum
of the nine individual support masses was wrong; bare support takes a minimum.

  A[nu]=sum d_j=.004658208018472682650444>0.

Let t=1/m, c_ji=c_light sqrt(M_i E_j*10^-6/2), so w_ji=c_ji t+c_ji/M_i.

    =sum_(j,i) d_j W_i(E_j)/nu(E_j)

This is continuous piecewise affine in (t,u). Its break lines are
  u=w_ji(t)-a_E, u=w_ji(t)+a_E.
On every cell of their arrangement in the rectangle
  1/6.0233 <=t<=1/5.764, 400<=u<=544,

rectangle corners, line-boundary intersections, and line-line intersections.
Their coordinates and the values of (19) use only radicals and Arb arithmetic.
Parallel lines are handled explicitly; any uncertain boundary inclusion is
retained conservatively. The 425 checked vertices all satisfy
  (19) < -3.24093035225.
This proves the sign on the whole mass-speed rectangle, not a grid of masses.

For u<=400 every positive-coefficient observation has all w_ji>632=u+a_E's
largest possible value, throughout the mass interval. Its kernel is zero;
the remaining coefficients are negative, so the functional is nonpositive.
The u=0 shell is included by this same argument. Integrating over any dF>=0
contradicts A[nu]>0. Every m in [5.764,6.0233] is excluded.

For m<5.764 the lightest isotope already has v_min(3.5)>S, since its exact
support mass is 5.76410768911... GeV. In this small-mass range lambda_i
increases with M_i, so all isotopes are kinematically absent there. This
excludes all lower masses. At the upper side Section 1 gives 7.793213... .
Thus M(.51,3.5) is contained in (6.0233,7.79321301728673).

No nonempty-set conclusion follows. This certificate does not determine an
infimum of a set whose nonemptiness has not been proved. Finite shell tests
certify separating functionals; passing finitely many tests does not by
itself establish continuous-spectrum membership. In general, finite point
samples cannot suffice: a smooth bump supported between sample energies
leaves all those observations unchanged while it can violate the local
differential inequality. A finite iff for this particular one-parameter
target family would require an additional theorem; none is asserted here.

4.2 Five keV.

5. TASK C(3): CRITICAL THRESHOLDS MUST BE INDEXED BY THE CUTOFF
-----------------------------------------------------------

Define, for fixed E_cut,
  E_c(E_cut)=inf{e: 0<e<E_cut and M(e,E_cut) is nonempty}.
Increasing e only removes constraints, so the set of thresholds admitting
some member is upward closed. No assumption that the mass membership set
is an interval is made. The existence or nonexistence of a member at the
infimum itself is also not assumed.

5.1 Full-endpoint exclusion for every threshold <=4.45.

Let isotope 0 be 124Xe, y(E)=eta(lambda_0 sqrt(E)),
z_i=(lambda_i/lambda_0)^2, T(E)=nu(E)/W_0(E), r_i(E)=W_i(E)/W_0(E).
For m in [6.65,27], all z_i increase with i. At E_b the target is zero
and all weights positive, so y(E_b)=0. Nonnegativity and monotonicity imply
y(E)=0 for E>=E_b. On the observed interval
  y(E)=T(E)-sum_(i=1)^8 r_i(E)y(z_i E).              (20)

Choose Bcut>1 such that
  max(z_3,z_1^2)<Bcut<min(z_4,z_1^3,z_1 z_2),
and put E0=E_b/Bcut. The only dilation words with product<Bcut are
empty, (1), (2), (3), (1,1). Substitution in (20) therefore terminates exactly:
  y(E0)=T(E0)-r_1(E0)T(z_1 E0)
       +r_1(E0)r_1(z_1 E0)T(z_1^2 E0)
       -r_2(E0)T(z_2 E0)-r_3(E0)T(z_3 E0).          (21)
Every omitted word is terminal zero; this is not a truncated Neumann series.

The script partitions the rational mass interval [6.65,27] adaptively into
1355 cells. On each cell, at its exact rational midpoint, set
  B_low=max(z_3,z_1^2), B_high=min(z_4,z_1^3,z_1 z_2),
  Bcut=(B_low+9B_high)/10.
Use that SAME Bcut for every mass in the cell. Arb checks the ordering,
the strict word bounds, E0>4.45, and negativity of (21) on the ENTIRE cell.
Across all cells, E0 is between 4.45026031083 and 4.50552153059 keV and
  (21) < -4.27537773256e-10.
Thus every such mass would require a negative halo value in the measured
interval [4.45,E_b], an impossibility.

For m<6.65, the lightest-isotope endpoint support mass is
6.68661931713...>6.65 and every heavier isotope is faster, so kinematics
excludes equality near E_b. For m>27, apply (1)--(4) at E=4.45:
  p=.47084119562208..., r=.13029928560243...,
  G>98090.54, max_i m_hi,i(4.45)<27.
Strict inequalities hold on the certified neighborhood of that energy.
These three mass ranges cover all m>0. This proves emptiness at threshold
4.45 and, by restriction, at every smaller threshold.

5.2 An explicit member at threshold 4.58.

Take m=7 GeV. Above E=4.58 all neutrino components except 124Xe vanish,
since their largest endpoint (126Xe) is 4.56539251605... keV. The shell
construction of Section 3.1 for the 124Xe target on [4.58,E_b] is valid:
  w1=738.41800058155...>a_E,
  w2=743.16238655402...<S.
It produces an atom-free nonnegative halo supported in Galactic speeds
u<=w2-a_E, hence eta(w)=0 for w>=w2. Every heavier isotope has
  lambda_i sqrt(4.58)>w2,
as certified by 4.58>E_b/z_i; the largest E_b/z_i is
4.57369479730... for 126Xe. Therefore every heavier DM component is zero
throughout the interval too. Scale eta by Q_124^2/A_124^2. Its 124Xe rate
is exactly the surviving neutrino target. Both spectra are zero beyond E_b,
so this also realizes equality up to any cutoff >=E_b, including 5 keV.

Combining 5.1 and 5.2 gives 4.45<=E_c(full)<=4.58. The nine single-isotope
full-endpoint critical thresholds are 2U^2/M_i*(a_E/S)^2, ranging from
.3780255665 to .4146492619 keV. Hence the mixture's exact full-endpoint
critical threshold cannot equal that of any one isotope.

The obstruction is a pointwise endpoint effect. Binned or tolerance-based
cone tests can suppress it and define a different critical threshold. They
cannot place the exact full-endpoint threshold between .35 and .40 keV.

5.3 A genuine, deliberately non-sharp bracket when E_cut=3.5.

For E_thr<=.3128, the functional in Section 4 excludes m<=6.0233 using
observations at E>=.51, which remain inside the measured interval. Formula
(1) at E=.3128 gives a strict separator, and
  max_i m_hi,i(.3128)<6.0233.
Thus all larger masses are excluded too: M(.3128,3.5) is empty.

For completeness, the following exact local construction proves nonemptiness
at threshold 3.499, without claiming anything at threshold .51. Fix m=7,
x0=sqrt(3.499), x1=sqrt(3.5), xc=sqrt(3.4995), t=.0831753 (km/s)^(-1).
The nine speed bands [lambda_i x0,lambda_i x1] are disjoint and ordered.
Define
  h_i=A0 exp[-t(lambda_i-lambda_0)xc],
  A0=nu(xc^2)/sum_i W_i(xc^2) exp[-t(lambda_i-lambda_0)xc].
On eight bands, all except isotope p=132, prescribe
  eta(lambda_i x)=h_i[1-t lambda_i(x-xc)].
On the remaining band DEFINE the function exactly by
  eta(lambda_p x)=[nu(x^2)-sum_(i!=p) W_i(x^2)
                                h_i(1-t lambda_i(x-xc))]/W_p(x^2).      (22)
Thus the mixture rate equals nu identically on the energy interval by
construction. Equation (22) uses the exact table integrals, not a fit.

The script certifies positivity, negative first derivative, and positive
second derivative of (22) over 100 adjacent energy cells covering the
whole interval. The minimum certified x-curvature is >.00327336 in the
raw normalization. The affine bands are also positive and decreasing.
For each gap, let s_left and s_right be the endpoint speed slopes, and let
s_sec be the secant slope between the endpoint values. All eight satisfy
  s_left < s_sec < s_right <0.
Connect the values by that affine segment. The slope jumps upward at both
ends, so the complete eta is continuous, decreasing and convex. Continue
its final slope until zero at 684.61671303669... km/s<S, then keep it zero.
Its lowest prescribed speed exceeds a_E. Section 3.1's inversion, interpreted
as a Stieltjes measure and allowing the upward convex kinks, constructs a
finite nonnegative isotropic speed measure realizing this entire eta on
the prescribed bands. Its derivative is a nonincreasing nonnegative q_nu;
the R-series and the terminal value again give exact equality.

Therefore M(3.499,3.5) contains m=7, giving
  .3128<=E_c(3.5)<=3.499.
This bracket is not a proof of the reported narrow binned bracket and is
not advertised as sharp. The full-endpoint threshold in 5.1--5.2 and this
truncated threshold are different mathematical quantities. No equality
with an individual isotope's critical threshold is proved for the latter.


6. AUDIT NOTES, REPRODUCIBILITY, AND REFERENCES
----------------------------------------------

The numerical constants displayed in prose are rounded summaries; the
inequalities asserted in the script and the balls in results.json are the
certificate. The same script also reruns the original supplied fixed-p,r
exclusions, original 6.0233 counterexample, and original .30 keV exclusion
as a nested baseline section. Those are cross-checks, not imported results.
The new proof above is self-contained and does not require trusting that
older results.json or form_factor_variations.json.

The program uses exact decimal inputs as balls, rational mass subdivisions,
and segment-integral enclosures. The only float conversion inherited from
the original integration routine proposes a table segment; rigorous ball
comparisons verify it, and neighboring pieces are enclosed at a knot. No
floating-point proposal is accepted as a sign certificate. All isotope
and skin ranges, mass cells, q cells, and line-arrangement vertices are
covered, including boundaries. The input checksum is mandatory.

Finite precision of Arb is numerical enclosure error only. Physical input
uncertainty is covered only when explicitly ranged (e.g. skin d in [0,.3]).
The theorem under positive spherical densities is conditional on those
density and radius hypotheses. It excludes anisotropic Galactic halos,
different DM proton/neutron couplings, and changed nuclear response terms
unless their own derivative/radius bounds are supplied.

Source of the external radius-model conventions, not a substitute for the
proof: D. Aristizabal Sierra, J. Liao, D. Marfatia, 'Impact of form factor
uncertainties on interpretations of coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus
scattering data', JHEP 06 (2019) 141, arXiv:1902.07398, especially Eqs.
(7),(8),(16) and Table I. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.07398
The paper uses .3 fm as a model envelope, not an experimental xenon-skin
measurement. The signed weak amplitude in (12) is the standard combination
of its neutron/proton amplitudes, with the weak-angle input retained from
the supplied certificate rather than changed to the paper's convention.

The Helm/Engel and low-q definitions evaluated in Section 1.3 are exactly
those specified in the supplied form_factor_variations.json. The nominal
Helm radius prescription is the Lewin--Smith one already encoded in the
input script. No claim of independent calibration or model uncertainty
coverage is attached to those named variations.

Final limitation: the requested lower-edge existence bracket at
[.51,3.5] remains an unsolved part of this response. Neither the strengthened
exclusion nor the exact member on [3.499,3.5] establishes it. The files make
