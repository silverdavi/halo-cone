NATURAL XENON: CERTIFIED EXCLUSIONS AND THE REMAINING EXISTENCE PROBLEM
===================================================================

Scope. This document proves (A), proves the upper bound requested in (B1),
disproves the proposed sufficiency assertion (B2), and proves an explicit
non-sharp threshold exclusion for (B3). It does NOT determine the exact
truncated membership set, prove its nonemptiness, prove that it is an interval,
or identify its sharp upper edge or sharp critical threshold.

Reproduce: install python-flint==0.9.0; run python certify.py.
The computation uses Arb real-ball arithmetic at 192-bit midpoint precision.
Ball radii enclose rounding and transcendental-function errors. It uses exact
analytic integrals on each spectrum segment, not numerical quadrature. Every
mass-cell test covers the entire cell, not only its endpoints or midpoint.

INPUTS

and Galactic/lab speeds in km/s. c_light=299792.458 km/s; a=232; V=544;
S=V+a=776. hbar*c=0.1973269804 GeV fm.

A=(124,126,128,129,130,131,132,134,136).

Nuclear M_i=atomic_i*0.93149410242-54*0.00051099895.
Abundances are precisely the nine numbers in the question, divided by their sum.
Q_i=A_i-54-(1-4*0.23857)*54.
Helm radius R_i^2=(1.23*A_i^(1/3)-0.60)^2+7*pi^2*0.52^2/3-5*0.9^2.
H_i(E)=[3(sin z-z cos z)/z^3]^2 exp[-(p*0.9)^2],

The central spectrum column is the continuous piecewise-linear interpolant,
with raw integral 0.999986544, renormalized to integral one. Its support ends
at U=16.36 MeV; 16.34 is the last positive node, not the zero endpoint.
SHA256 b07b3ba052c08cf675c8fcc5c5f674e0946390b721a0ea4da719077bdf5bc892.
This is the supplied Bahcall table, not an independently supplied Winter table.

Set

There is no flux, weak-interaction, exposure, or mean-nuclear-mass multiplier.
The freely adjustable positive normalization can be absorbed into the halo,
so equality is always written D=N. Exclusion also applies to nonnegative
speed measures, a larger class than ordinary speed densities.

THEOREM A: THE STRICT SET AT 0.51 keV IS EMPTY

Index isotopes from 0=124Xe. Define
 lambda_i=c_light*sqrt(M_i*10^-6/2)*(1/m+1/M_i),
 z_i=alpha_i^2=(M_0/M_i)*[(m+M_i)/(m+M_0)]^2,
 E_b=2*U^2/M_0=4.639042693471716638... keV,
 rho=0.98, E_0=rho^2 E_b=4.455336602810236659... keV,
 y(E)=eta(lambda_0 sqrt(E)), T(E)=N(E)/W_0(E),
 r_i(E)=W_i(E)/W_0(E).

At E_b, N(E_b)=0 and all W_i are positive. Hence y(E_b)=0; since eta is
nonnegative and nonincreasing, y(E)=0 for all E>=E_b. On the measured interval,
  y(E)=T(E)-sum_{i=1}^8 r_i(E)y(z_i E).                 (A.1)
No physical support assumption beyond nonnegativity and monotonicity is needed
to propagate this terminal zero.

The following inequalities suffice for the five-term termination:
  1<alpha_1<alpha_2<...<alpha_8,
  max(alpha_3,alpha_1^2)<1/rho,
  min(alpha_4,alpha_1^3,alpha_1 alpha_2)>1/rho.          (A.2)
The only words whose product of dilations is below 1/rho are then
  empty, (1), (2), (3), (1,1).
Indeed every one-letter word beyond 3 is too large; every two-letter word
other than (1,1) is at least alpha_1 alpha_2; every word of length three or
more is at least alpha_1^3. Repeated substitution in (A.1) therefore gives
  y(E_0)=T(E_0)-r_1(E_0)T(z_1 E_0)
       +r_1(E_0)r_1(z_1 E_0)T(z_1^2 E_0)
       -r_2(E_0)T(z_2 E_0)-r_3(E_0)T(z_3 E_0).        (A.3)

For the stated normalization, the five terms at m=7 GeV are approximately
  +5.46526348709344544e-10
  -9.62458711533917540e-11
  +1.35261809321223299e-11
  -3.20592427696401344e-10
  -6.35523906130690073e-10,
with certified sum -4.9230967533901629738292081575689...e-10.
The previous -1.92831...e-10 used an additional overall rate normalization;
the value is scale dependent, while its sign is not.

The code adaptively partitions the EXACT rational interval [6.65,7.80] into
39 cells, verifies (A.2) throughout each cell, and evaluates (A.3) by ball
arithmetic throughout each cell. The resulting global upper bound is
  y(E_0)<-2.15e-10.
This is an interval certificate, not the earlier high-precision agreement test.
The sharper previous uniform numerical bound is not needed or claimed here.

For m<6.65, all isotope speeds near E_b exceed S: in this mass range lambda_i
increases with M_i, and the lightest-isotope endpoint condition requires
  m >= 6.6866193171333994309... GeV.
Thus D vanishes on a terminal open interval where N is positive. For m>7.80,
Theorem B1 below applies, since max_i m_hi_i=7.793213017286722501... GeV.
Together these exclusions cover every positive m. QED.

THEOREM B1: WEIGHTED CURVATURE, WITHOUT A SMALL-DILATION ASSUMPTION

Let x=sqrt(E), write H_i(x)=H_i(x^2), and ell_i=log H_i. Let p,r be constants.
If on an open x interval
  2 ell_i'+p >=0,
  ell_i''+(ell_i')^2+p ell_i'+r <=0                   (B.1)
for every isotope, and lambda_i x<a for every isotope, then
  L D <=0 distributionally, L=d^2/dx^2+p d/dx+r.      (B.2)

Proof. Put g_i(x)=eta(lambda_i x). Then g_i>=0, g_i'<=0 and g_i''<=0
distributionally on this interval. Product differentiation gives
  L[W_i g_i]=W_i[g_i''+(2 ell_i'+p)g_i'
                    +(ell_i''+(ell_i')^2+p ell_i'+r)g_i]<=0.
Sum over isotopes. This proof requires no bound on alpha_i-1 or Q_i^2/A_i^2.
For this target the certificate additionally checks L[H_i Psi(q_i)]/H_i>0
for every isotope separately, so the weak-charge spread requires no smallness
condition either. QED.

At threshold 0.51, choose p=0.146 keV^-1/2 and r=0.092 keV^-1. Throughout
E in [0.51,0.510000001] keV, the interval computation verifies
  min_i(2 ell_i'+p)>0.000690,
  max_i(ell_i''+(ell_i')^2+p ell_i'+r)<-0.00123,
  L N>1.006e6
in the above raw normalization. The full balls are in results.json.
In fact min_i L[H_i Psi(q_i)]/H_i>1.3700 on the same interval.

Define mu_hi_i=c_light*sqrt(M_i*E_thr*10^-6/2)/a and
  m_hi_i=M_i*mu_hi_i/(M_i-mu_hi_i).
The extreme values at E_thr=.51 are
  m_hi_124=7.4628366000548516566... GeV,
  m_hi_136=7.7932130172867225011... GeV.
Every isotope speed at threshold is below a if m>m_hi_136. Since each speed
decreases with m, this remains true for arbitrarily large m even when the
ordering of isotope speeds changes.
For such m define
  Delta=min(10^-9, min_i[(a/lambda_i)^2-.51])/2>0.
On (.51,.51+Delta), also shortened to E_cut when necessary, (B.2) contradicts
L N>0. Therefore, for every E_cut>.51,
  M(.51,E_cut) is a subset of (0,7.7932130172867225011...].
No sufficiency or sharpness is inferred from this bound. The reported binned
edge 8.14 GeV is not an edge for equality of continuous spectra.

LEMMA: FINITE POINT FUNCTIONALS HAVE AN EXACT FINITE SHELL TEST

For fixed observed energies E_j and coefficients d_j, define
  A[G]=sum_j d_j G(E_j)/N(E_j).
Let K_m(E,u)=sum_i W_i(E)k(lambda_i sqrt(E),u) be a boosted-shell rate.
For each fixed E, on every u interval excluding the breakpoints
  0, V, |lambda_i sqrt(E)-a|, lambda_i sqrt(E)+a,
the function K_m(E,u) is of the form A+B/u. Consequently the same holds for
A[K_m(.,u)], whose maximum on such an interval is attained at an endpoint.
Checking the finitely many relevant breakpoints proves its sign for all u>0.
The u=0 shell is checked separately, or by an explicit low-u argument.
If A[K_m(.,u)]<=0 for all u while A[N]>0, no nonnegative speed measure can
produce N. This follows immediately by integrating the inequality against F.

THEOREM B2: THE PROPOSED SUFFICIENCY STATEMENT IS FALSE

At E_thr=.51, E_cut=3.5, all individual endpoint-support conditions hold for
  m=6.0233 GeV,
because their largest required mass is
  max_i m_support_i(3.5)=6.0232769564069224721... GeV.
Nevertheless m=6.0233 is NOT in M(.51,3.5).

Certificate. Let E_j=.51+2.99*j/99 keV. Use the exact decimal coefficients
  j     d_j
   0   -0.0000001
  86   -2.5091846054218256e-08
  89   +4.232602196532087e-07
  92   -3.12282552577074e-06
  93   -5.49029821603542e-05
  94   -0.0029039370021078214
  97   +0.04322536788188634
  98   -0.49420890705022336
  99   +0.45960331182823005.
Then
  A[N]=sum_j d_j=0.005658108018472682650444>0.
For u<=400, every positive-coefficient observation has all isotope speeds
greater than 632=u_bound+a, so its kernel is zero; A[K]<=0.
For u in [400,544], the code checks every relevant breakpoint (83 including
the two interval boundaries), obtaining
  A[K_m(.,u)] < -1.81e-9.
The lemma proves the exclusion for all shells, hence all speed measures. QED.

The necessity claimed for the proposed lower edge also does not follow from
support: a positive isotope SUM needs at least one contributing isotope, not
every isotope. Bare support gives a necessary minimum over isotopes, equal
to 5.7641076891152682379... GeV at E_cut=3.5. It is not sufficient.

Even for one isotope, finite-cutoff support is not the whole extension
condition. If w_c>V-a, then eta is convex above w_c and
  eta(w_c) <= (S-w_c)(-eta'(w_c)).                    (B.3)
Proof: for w>V-a each contributing shell is (u+a-w)_+/(2au), and
(u+a-w)_+ <= (S-w) 1_{u+a>w}; integrate.
For the target Psi(mu w), this requires
  mu >= [q_c+Psi(q_c)/(2h(q_c))]/S
in consistent natural units. The additional positive term disappears only
at a true zero endpoint. This illustrates the lost terminal data at E_cut.

Small dilations alone do not supply a Neumann-series proof. Translation by
any nonzero distance on bounded continuous functions on R differs from the
identity by operator norm 2; in log velocity, dilation is translation. A
contraction requires an additional norm, regularity estimates and positivity
margins, none of which follow from |alpha_i-1| being small for all admissible
halos. The certificate above directly rules out the proposed universal claim.

THEOREM B3: AN EXPLICIT, NON-SHARP CRITICAL-THRESHOLD EXCLUSION

For E_cut=3.5 and every E_thr<=0.30 keV,
  M(E_thr,3.5) is empty.

Proof. If m<5.764, bare support excludes equality at 3.5. For the entire
interval 5.764<=m<=5.893 use the preceding finite-point lemma with only
  d_0=-0.0000001,
  d_93=-0.18393443965754055,
  d_99=+0.8160655603424595.
Its target value is 0.63213102068491895>0. The code verifies its shell sign
throughout this mass interval using five interval cells. All these energy
points lie in [E_thr,3.5].

For m>5.893 every isotope is below a at .30, because
  max_i m_hi_i(.30)=5.8925713382733308205...<5.893.
At .30 choose p=.113 keV^-1/2 and r=.089 keV^-1. On
[.30,.300000001], the code verifies (B.1) and L N>6.53e5, so the local
curvature obstruction applies to every remaining mass. QED.

This establishes emptiness below .30; it does not locate the exact critical
threshold. In particular no assertion of membership just above .30 is made.

CUTOFF DEPENDENCE AND THE EXACT GAP

Raising E_cut or lowering E_thr can only shrink the membership set. Thus the
6.0233 counterexample and the .30-keV empty-set theorem also apply when the
upper cut is raised above 3.5. Theorem B1 is local and applies to every
E_cut>E_thr at the specified threshold. Theorem A uses observation of the
actual zero endpoint E_b and does not establish emptiness at E_cut=3.5.

For a truncated interval, (A.1) has unknown terminal values: y(E) need not
vanish above E_cut. Substituting zero there changes the problem. Solving for
those terminal values subject to the global tail-positivity/equality
constraints is the remaining existence problem. Neither a grid-feasible
halo nor the upper-edge differential inequality solves it.

Unproved: the exact M(.51,3.5), existence at any specified mass on that
interval, connectedness of M, the sharp upper edge, and the sharp critical
threshold. This package contains exclusion certificates only, with an
explicit counterexample to the requested sufficiency statement.
